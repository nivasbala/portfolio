# Product Management Lifecycle: An overview

## Introduction

Product Management Lifecycle unlike Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC) is more of an art than science where adaptability is the key to success. When building a products the stages in the product management lifecyle will vary by company, staff, maturity, product type, business model etc.,

As a product manager, it is very important to understand the current situation of the company and various factors when coming up with a product management lifecycle that fits your company, product and market (customer problem).

This blog attempts to organize around what a typical product management lifecycle would look like from a Product Manager's perspective. While some of the stages illustrated are essential and cannot be skipped, there will be a lot of variability to what is illustrated here. This blog will hopefully provide a guideline when you start your journey of building products that solve customer problems.

## 1. What is Product Management?

If ask chatgpt or google the old fashioned way you will get various definitions. Here is a simple one, **"Product Management is the process of building product(s) that solve customer problem(s) and make money for the company"**. There are various stages involved in building building and launching a product, like ideation, development, go to market etc.,

Product Managers play a crucial part in orchestrating this product lifecycle. Of course nothing can be achieved without the talented engineers and designers, sales, marketing, customer support and all the other functions involved.

There are various skills needed by a product manager such as understanding the problem correctly, understadning the market, defining the scope (prioritzing), communication, strategic thinking, creative problem-solving etc., (I will write about this later)

One Key skill to understand the context of the compnay and coming up with the product management lifecycle. In smaller companies and startups this process is not clearly defined and it evolves. Having this defined would be immensly helpful. This is what you will find in this blog.

## 2. The Stages of the Product Management Lifecycle

The Stages are
- 1. Define Company's Mission and Vision
- 2. Identify Customer Problem - Get Customer Insights
- 3. Do Financial Analysis (TAM, SAM, SOM)
- 4. Define Business Model
- 5. Strategic Product Planning (Optional)
- 6. Product Opportunity Analysis
- 7. Discovering the Product (MVP Creation + UI/UX Design)
- 8. Identify, Define, Scope out "The Whole Product Offer"
- 9. Define and Execute the Goto Market Strategy
- 10. Product Growth and Evolution

The Fishbone timeline map illustrates this process. Lets dive into the stages

### 2.1 Define Mission and Vision

### A. Idea Generation and Discovery
- Identifying customer needs or market gaps.
- Methods for idea generation (customer feedback, market research, competitor analysis).
- Tools and techniques for brainstorming and validating ideas (e.g., customer interviews, surveys, data analysis).
- **How aspiring PMs can demonstrate experience:** Show how you’ve helped identify pain points or explored new product opportunities in previous roles, internships, or side projects.

### B. Product Strategy and Planning
- Defining the product vision and goals.
- Aligning the product strategy with company objectives.
- Conducting SWOT analysis and competitive research.
- How to create a product roadmap and prioritize features.
- **How aspiring PMs can demonstrate experience:** Talk about your ability to define goals, prioritize, and align team efforts toward a common vision. Example: managing a project or product roadmap in a non-PM role.

### C. Design and Development
- Collaborating with designers and engineers to turn ideas into product features.
- Writing user stories, acceptance criteria, and working on wireframes/prototypes.
- Communicating with stakeholders throughout the process.
- **How aspiring PMs can demonstrate experience:** Illustrate how you worked with cross-functional teams, either in direct product design or project management roles.

### D. Testing and Validation
- Conducting user testing (beta testing, A/B testing, usability testing).
- Collecting feedback and refining the product.
- Analyzing product metrics and key performance indicators (KPIs) to assess user satisfaction and product success.
- **How aspiring PMs can demonstrate experience:** Reference any experience with product testing, user feedback collection, or data-driven decision-making.

### E. Launch and Go-to-Market Strategy
- Coordinating with marketing, sales, and customer support for product launch.
- Defining launch goals and success metrics.
- Post-launch monitoring and iteration based on initial customer feedback.
- **How aspiring PMs can demonstrate experience:** Share examples of any product launch you have been involved in, even if you had a limited role. Discuss your role in coordinating efforts across teams.

### F. Post-Launch and Iteration
- Collecting and analyzing ongoing user feedback and performance data.
- Iterating on the product (bug fixes, new features, adjustments based on market needs).
- Continuously improving the product and scaling.
- **How aspiring PMs can demonstrate experience:** Highlight examples where you took initiative to improve a product or service based on user feedback or market insights.

## 4. The Challenges of Product Management

- **Balancing Stakeholder Expectations:** Managing different, sometimes conflicting, interests from leadership, customers, and teams.
- **Prioritization:** Deciding which features or improvements should be prioritized when resources are limited.
- **Communication:** Ensuring clear and consistent communication between cross-functional teams.
- **Adapting to Change:** Navigating shifting market conditions, customer demands, or organizational goals.
- **How aspiring PMs can demonstrate experience:** Share stories of how you've handled challenges, prioritization dilemmas, or communication issues in previous roles.
